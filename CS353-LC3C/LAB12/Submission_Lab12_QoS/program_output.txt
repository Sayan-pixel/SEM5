--- Part A Written Answers ---
Question 4a: Which flow's guarantee does the rejection protect?
Answer:
  1. Primary: Rejection directly protects f4's OWN contract guarantee, because the network
     cannot fulfill f4's requested service level (3 Mbps) under the committed fair-share rate (2 Mbps).
  2. Existing flows: If f4 were forcefully granted 3 Mbps (requiring weight W >= 12/7 ≈ 1.714),
     the total requested bandwidth (2 + 2 + 4 + 3 = 11 Mbps) would exceed the physical link capacity
     (10 Mbps). This would degrade the guaranteed rates of previously admitted flows f1, f2, and f3
     below their contractual commitments (e.g., at W_4 = 2, f1 and f2 get only 1.67 Mbps < 2 Mbps,
     and f3 gets only 3.33 Mbps < 4 Mbps). Hence, rejection protects flows f1, f2, and f3.
Question 4b: What weight or rate would f4 need to be admitted?
Answer:
  - Rate requirement: With its given weight W_4 = 1, total weight is ΣW = 4 + 1 = 5.
    f4 is guaranteed (1/5) * 10 = 2.0 Mbps. Therefore, f4 would be admitted if its requested rate
    is reduced to R_4 <= 2.0 Mbps.
  - Weight note: No weight alone can admit f4 at R_4 = 3.0 Mbps because the sum of requested rates
    (2 + 2 + 4 + 3 = 11 Mbps) strictly exceeds link capacity C = 10 Mbps. The link has only
    10 - (2 + 2 + 4) = 2.0 Mbps of unallocated capacity.

Question 3: Explain why exactly two packets are dropped, in terms of bucket capacity.
Answer:
  The bucket capacity is b = 4 tokens, and it starts full. At time t = 0, a burst of 6 packets
  arrives simultaneously (Δt = 0), meaning zero new tokens accrue during the burst.
  Each packet consumes L = 1 token. The first 4 packets consume all 4 tokens in the bucket,

Question 3b: State the single difference between shaping and policing that produces drops in one and delays in the other.
Answer:
  The single fundamental difference is the presence of a PACKET BUFFER (QUEUE).
  - A policer has NO buffer: non-conforming packets are immediately dropped (or marked) upon arrival.
  - A shaper has a BUFFER: non-conforming packets are held in a queue and delayed until sufficient tokens
    accrue, trading buffer latency for zero packet loss.

--- Part D Written Answers ---
Question 4a: Compare the two orders and explain how the weight-2 flow is served.
Comparison:
  - WFQ Order:  A, C, B, E, F, G, D, H
  - FIFO Order: A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H
Explanation of how Flow 3 (weight = 2) is served:
  - In WFQ, a flow's finish number increments by length / weight.
    Because Flow 3 has weight 2 (twice that of Flow 1 and Flow 2), its virtual transmission time
    per byte is cut in half (10/2 = 5 for C, 8/2 = 4 for E, 10/2 = 5 for G).
  - Consequently, even though Packet C (length 10) arrives at time 5 alongside Packet B (length 6),
    Packet C's finish number is 5 + 10/2 = 10, whereas Packet B's finish number is 5 + 6/1 = 11.
    Therefore, Packet C is scheduled BEFORE Packet B!
  - Similarly, Packet E (arrival 8, length 8) finishes at 10 + 8/2 = 14, jumping ahead of Flow 1's
    Packet F (finish 16) and Flow 2's Packet D (arrival 8, length 9, finish 20).
  - Under FIFO, Flow 3 would be stuck behind earlier arrivals or equal-arrival ties regardless of weight.
    WFQ ensures Flow 3 receives exactly twice the bandwidth share of the link compared to Flows 1 and 2.

Question 4b: How does Weighted Fair Queuing protect a flow from being starved by another?
Answer:
  - In FIFO, an aggressive or misbehaving flow that floods packets can monopolize the queue and link,
    causing severe delay or complete starvation for all other flows.
  - In WFQ, each flow maintains its own virtual queue state. A packet's finish number depends on the
    PREVIOUS finish number of that same flow (max(arrival, prev_finish) + length / weight).
  - If an aggressive flow transmits a massive burst of packets, its finish numbers advance far into
    the future (e.g., F1, F2 = F1 + L/w, F3 = F2 + L/w, ...).
  - Meanwhile, an idle or well-behaved flow that suddenly sends a packet will have its finish number
    computed starting from its current arrival time (max(arrival, 0) + length/weight), resulting in a
    much smaller finish number. The scheduler immediately serves the conforming flow's packet ahead
    of the backlog of the aggressive flow.
  - Thus, WFQ provides strict FLOW ISOLATION: every flow i is mathematically guaranteed a minimum service
    rate of (W_i / ΣW) * C over any active period, making starvation impossible.
