1. TTL and Checksum: The Time to Live (TTL) field changes at every router. 
Because the header checksum is a mathematical sum over the entire IPv4 header, 
altering the TTL field invalidates the original sum. Therefore, the router must recompute 
the checksum at every hop before forwarding the packet. In the corrupted packet from Part B, 
the TTL was altered from 0x40 to 0x3F, but the checksum was left unchanged, causing the 
computed checksum (0xDC10) to mismatch the stored checksum (0xDB10).

2. Fragment Offset Multiples: The fragment offset field is strictly measured in units of 8 
bytes. Because receivers reconstruct the payload by multiplying this offset by 8, every 
fragment except the final one must carry a payload whose length is a multiple of 8 bytes to 
ensure the subsequent fragment aligns cleanly on an 8-byte boundary.

3. Checksum Coverage: The IPv4 checksum covers only the header, leaving the payload out of 
the calculation. This design optimizes processing speeds at intermediate routers, as 
computing a checksum over large payloads at every network hop would introduce severe latency. 
The payload is instead protected by the checksums of higher-layer protocols (like TCP or UDP), 
which are evaluated only by the destination hosts.
