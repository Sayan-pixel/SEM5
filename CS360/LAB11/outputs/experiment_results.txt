OBSERVATIONS & ANALYSIS:
========================================================================================
1. Necessity of Z-Score Standardization (Q1.b):
   Standardization is indispensable. Pollutants operate on vastly different baselines
   (e.g., PM10 values reach >150 microgram/m3, whereas SO2 values rarely exceed 25 microgram/m3).
   Without standardization, the Euclidean distance in DBSCAN would be dominated entirely by PM10,
   rendering gaseous pollutants (NO2, SO2) completely inert.

2. Impact of Varying eps (Settings 1, 2, 3):
   - Small eps (0.5): Extremely stringent neighborhood radius; 34.0% of points are marked as noise.
     Produces 3 compact, dense clusters.
   - Moderate eps (1.2): Merges nearby high-density regions into 1 large macro-cluster (0 noise points).
   - Large eps (2.1): All points easily fall within eps radius, collapsing into a single cluster.

3. Impact of Varying minPts (Settings 4, 5):
   Holding eps constant at 0.8, increasing minPts from 5 to 7 raises the core point density threshold,
   increasing the noise percentage from 12.0% to 22.0% as sparser boundary stations are dropped.

4. Silhouette Score around Noise Points (Q1.g):
   Silhouette Score cannot be computed meaningfully around noise points because noise (-1) represents
   unclustered anomalies rather than an actual cluster. In standard practice, noise points are filtered out
   prior to computing cluster compactness and separation.
