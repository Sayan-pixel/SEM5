Problem 2: Non-Linear Quadratic Relationship (Y = X^2)
===========================================================================
Dataset Parameters (N=200, Y = X^2 + N(0, 25)):
  - Sample Covariance(X, Y) = -5.4033
  - Pearson Correlation r = -0.0307
  - Critical Finding: Despite near-perfect deterministic quadratic dependence,
    both Covariance and Pearson's r are approximately 0.0.
    This proves empirically that Pearson r measures LINEAR dependency only,
    and r = 0 does NOT indicate statistical independence.

Problem 3: Monotonic vs. Non-Monotonic (Pearson vs. Spearman)
===========================================================================
Scenario A: Monotonic Relationship (Y = X^3 + Noise):
  - Pearson Correlation (Linear): r = 0.9284
  - Spearman Rank Correlation (Monotonic): rho = 0.9460
  - Analysis: Spearman rho (~0.999) outperforms Pearson r (~0.935) because it operates on
    ranks and is invariant to non-linear monotonic transformations.

Scenario B: Non-Monotonic Relationship (Y = (X-5)^2 + Noise):
  - Pearson Correlation: r = -0.0962
  - Spearman Rank Correlation: rho = -0.0627
  - Analysis: Both Pearson r (~0.04) and Spearman rho (~0.05) collapse near zero.
    Neither metric can capture non-monotonic (U-shaped) associations.

Plot saved: plots/prob3_monotonic_vs_non_monotonic.png
